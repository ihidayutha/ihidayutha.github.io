The earliest mention of "sack of saints" is in the Life of Theodotus of Amida (qv), a seventh-century aksenaya.

There were attempts by the Church to eradicate the practice during the Islamic period. One of the earliest legislative efforts is found in the set of rules for monks issued by George, Bishop of the Arab Tribes. In the first paragraph of rules, George orders that monks who carry bags of relics of saints and who travel around, will not be received. Later this rule is found in Canon 15 of the Canons of Patriarch George of Antioch who threatens excommunication for monks and priests who carry bags of the saints and travel around in the villages. Later, in the Canons of Patriarch Quriaqos of Tagrit, it is stated that not only monks and priests but also deacons and laymen uphold this custom.
